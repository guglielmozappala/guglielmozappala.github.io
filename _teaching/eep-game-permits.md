## The story

Your team runs a firm that emits six tonnes a year. A regulator has capped the market: every tonne emitted needs a permit, and there are fewer permits than tonnes. Whatever your firm cannot cover with permits it must abate, at a cost that rises with every tonne and that only your firm knows. You trade permits with the other firms in your market. Cheap abaters should end up selling to expensive abaters; whether that happens is up to you.

The room is split into two markets, A and B, with identical rules and four firms each. After the first round of trading the whole class becomes the regulator: you debate two amendments to the market's rules, vote one in, and a coin decides which of the two markets lives under it in the second round. The other market carries on unchanged. In Week 8 we treat that as an experiment and ask what your amendment did.

## How we play

- When I start the game, your firm is dealt at random: Alfa, Bravo, Charlie or Delta. Your page shows your private cost card, the cost of abating each tonne from the first to the sixth. Keep it private: your costs are your own, and nobody may reveal a card.
- Every firm starts with three free permits. The market has twelve.
- **Trading.** Post a buy or a sell order at a price of your choice, in whole euros, for one to six permits. If your order crosses one on the other side of the book it trades at once, at the resting order's price; otherwise it waits in the book until someone crosses it or you cancel it. You can only sell permits you hold. Nobody suggests a price: find it.
- **Compliance.** When the round closes you emit one tonne for every permit you hold, at most six, and abate the rest at your card's cost. A firm that holds more than six permits has wasted money. Abate a tonne if its cost is at or below the permit price: that rule is printed on every card.
- The clock runs for about 25 minutes. Halfway through I freeze both markets for a minute and ask you to note whether you would revise your numbers.

## Round 1

Both markets trade under the rules above. At the close we put each market's price, abatement, emissions and total abatement cost next to what the same firms would have paid if each had simply met the cap on its own.

## The committee

You are no longer a firm. Every student receives a two-page dossier with both markets' results, and the class debates two amendments to the market's rules. You will see them in class, not before. Blank ballot, simple majority, a casting vote if it is a tie. Before the result is opened, the committee writes and signs one sentence predicting what the winning amendment will do. Then the coin: heads, market A gets the amendment and market B carries on unchanged; tails, the other way round. The mapping is announced before the flip.

## Round 2

Abatement has become more expensive, for every firm in both markets by exactly the same amount: open your card. The treated market runs the amendment, the control market runs the original rules. Trade again.

## After class

The tape, every trade with its time, market, buyer, seller, price and quantity, goes on Blackboard within 24 hours, for both markets and both rounds. Week 8 opens on it: treatment and control, the difference the amendment made, and the threats to inference that you will have spotted yourselves.

## Tips

- Your reservation prices are on your card if you work them out: the most a permit is worth to you, the least you should accept for one. Do the arithmetic before you trade.
- Overbuying hurts: permits beyond your six tonnes are worthless.
- Watch the book and the tape, not just your own orders. The running price is information.
- Nothing about the outcome is graded. Trade as the firm you are.
